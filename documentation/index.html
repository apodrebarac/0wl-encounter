01 — Overview & timeline

A body of practice-led PhD research in which recurrent neural networks are trained on intimate personal archives and physiological data to generate poetry. The practice spans from the first text generator F0x (2017) to the current 0wl system — encompassing EEG-driven performances, tree-mapping walks across London, cross-species neural experiments, and iterative model development.

Each project builds on the last. The "0" in F0x carries forward through all models: a mark of the archive, the body, the loop that refuses to close cleanly.

January 2019

March–May 2019

02 — F0x — grandfather's archive (2017–2019)

RNNtextgenrnnNLTK

Concept

F0x was the first text generator in the practice — named for the "0" that would carry forward through all subsequent models. Trained on the translated and anonymised letters of the author's grandfather, it operated as a wounded archive: data shaped by grief, displacement, and the irreversibility of loss.

The model was intentionally kept fragile. Rather than being repaired when it began to fail, it was allowed to collapse — its final outputs (stutters, loops, blank sequences, ASCII ruins) treated as a form of algorithmic mourning. Collapse is not failure but knowledge; care sometimes requires allowing a system to unravel.

Corpus

  • SourcePersonal letters by the author's grandfather
  • LanguageOriginal (Croatian/Yugoslav) → translated to English
  • Pre-processingAnonymisation (names, locations removed); stemming and lemmatisation via NLTK

Model

  • ArchitectureRNN, character-level LSTM
  • Librarytextgenrnn (Python)
  • Training intentFragmentation and drift treated as meaningful, not corrected
from textgenrnn import textgenrnn
from nltk.stem import PorterStemmer, WordNetLemmatizer

# Pre-processing
stemmer = PorterStemmer()
lemmatizer = WordNetLemmatizer()

# Training

textgen.train_from_file('grandfather_corpus.txt', num_epochs=50)

Outputs

Fragmented poetry, increasingly incoherent over the model's lifetime. Final poem begins: "Devotion in a voice and processing of being..."

Ceiling installation (January 2019) — F0x's final outputs suspended in space as the model died.

Venice Biennale 2024 — Croatian Pavilion, within Vlatka Horvat and Antonia Majaca's project By the Means at Hand: a booklet of F0x's saved outputs exhibited as a quiet diary of diasporic adaptation and unease.

03 — Immigrant and a Tree — dual archive system (2019)

EEGtree mappingRNN₀ + RNN₁PLUX biosignalsplux

Concept

A dual mapping system in which the internal nervous system (brain / body / EEG) and the external nervous system (city / trees / displacement) were constructed simultaneously and trained together as a binary dataset. The two archives produced two models (RNN₀ and RNN₁) and two poetry books (Book 0 and Book 1 / am).

The work closed recursively: poetry generated by the RNNs was read aloud to the trees that had been assigned the original EEG fragments — returning machine-processed language to its non-human origin. The loop: body → EEG → writing → fragments → trees → RNN → poetry → voice → trees.

Word fragment extraction

After each four-hour session, the words and phrases that physically survived the endurance of continuous wall-inscription were gathered as residues. These were not algorithmically extracted — they were what remained after exhaustion: the linguistic equivalent of chalk dust. Examples: "displaced", "root", "still", "glass", "waiting", "breath", "foreign", "threshold", "tremor", "silence".

These fragments became the internal corpus — training data for RNN₁.

04 — EEG with my cat — Peerly × The Decameron (2020)

EEGPLUX biosignalspluxRNNBoccaccio

Concept

During COVID-19 lockdown in Croatia, living in isolation with the cat Peerly, EEG sensors were attached to map his three most consistent daily activities: eating, sleeping, and playing. Each neural spike triggered a word from Boccaccio's The Decameron (1353) — chosen because its context of plague, enforced isolation, storytelling, and survival mirrored the crisis unfolding outside.

The cat's quiet, cyclical rhythms became the generative engine for a body of poetry carrying traces of cross-species neural activity and fourteenth-century narrative.

05 — 0wl — grandmother's archive (ongoing)

RNN

Concept

0wl is the successor to F0x — where F0x was trained on the grandfather's archive, 0wl is trained on the grandmother's writing. The naming reflects a shift: F0x (fox, cunning, fleeting) gives way to 0wl (owl, patient, nocturnal, watching). Together they constitute a dual ancestral archive — two voices from the same displaced family, processed through the same recursive neural logic.

Corpus

  • SourceGrandmother's writing — translated and anonymised (consistent with F0x ethical framework)
  • ModelRNN (architecture consistent with F0x and dual-archive system)

08 — Conceptual principles

These principles have guided all technical choices and should inform future EEG and model work:

01

Fragmentation is data, not error

Incomplete outputs, stutters, and model collapse are meaningful outputs. Preserve them rather than correcting for them. The instability of the model mirrors the instability of the archive.

02

The body is a corpus

EEG data is not just signal — it is corporeal autobiography. Technical decisions (spike threshold, sampling rate, channel selection) shape what of the body is legible. Document every choice.

03

Temperature as poetics

Generation temperature is an aesthetic parameter as much as a technical one. Always generate at multiple temperatures. Document which temperature produced which poems and why each felt right.

04

Collapse as ethics

Models are not repaired or retrained when they begin to fail. Allowing collapse is a refusal of the logic of optimisation. The delay between input and output, the willingness to let a model die — these are refusals of instant gratification.

05

The loop must close

Each system returns its outputs to its origin: poetry is read back to trees; F0x's collapse is archived, not discarded; the machine's outputs re-enter the world that generated them. Design the return.

09 — Notes for future EEG work

Session setup checklist

  • Charge biosignalsplux device fully before session
  • Document electrode placement for each session (consistent 10-20 system or custom)
  • Run 2–3 minute baseline recording — used to calculate per-session mean and std
  • Enable channels 2, 3, 5 (established in 2019 sessions) or document any changes
  • Verify OpenSignals export format before long sessions

Training checklist

  • Corpus anonymised and ethically cleared
  • Corpus pre-processed (NLTK stemming / lemmatisation)
  • Baseline EEG recorded per session
  • Spike events extracted from all sessions
  • Spike-to-word mapping documented
  • Training run: num_epochs=50, rnn_size=128, rnn_layers=2
  • Outputs generated at t=0.7, t=1.2, t=1.5
  • All temperature outputs saved (including collapses)
  • Model weights saved (even if model later collapses)

Reusable session processing function

from opensignals import load_session
import numpy as np

def process_eeg_session(filepath, spike_multiplier=2.0, min_gap=50):
    """
    Load and process a single OpenSignals EEG session.

    filepath        : path to .txt OpenSignals export
    spike_multiplier: threshold = mean + multiplier * std
                      lower = more sensitive (more spikes detected)
                      higher = less sensitive (fewer, stronger spikes)
    min_gap         : minimum samples between spikes (onset detection)
    """
    eeg = load_session(filepath)

    threshold = np.mean(eeg) + spike_multiplier * np.std(eeg)
    spike_indices = np.where(eeg > threshold)[0]

    events = [s for i, s in enumerate(spike_indices)
              if i == 0 or s - spike_indices[i-1] > min_gap]

    return events

# Process all sessions
all_events = []
for session_file in session_files:

Corpus preparation function

import nltk
from nltk.stem import PorterStemmer, WordNetLemmatizer

def prepare_corpus(raw_text):
    """
    Pre-process text corpus for RNN training.
    Consistent with F0x and dual-archive methodology.
    """
    stemmer = PorterStemmer()
    lemmatizer = WordNetLemmatizer()

    tokens = nltk.word_tokenize(raw_text.lower())
    processed = [lemmatizer.lemmatize(t) for t in tokens]

    return ' '.join(processed)

10 — Final 0wl model — training pipeline

The final 0wl is trained in four layers, each archive absorbed in sequence. This preserves each voice's weight without any single corpus drowning the others. The model file is at 0wl-model/text_generator.py in the repository.

The four archives

Running the full pipeline

A single shell script runs the entire sequence. It skips any archive whose file isn't present yet and picks up from where it left off:

cd 0wl-model
bash run_0wl.sh

Or run each step manually:

# Step 1 — base model on books 0+1
python3 text_generator.py train \
  --input-text ../corpus.txt \
  --checkpoint checkpoints/0wl_base.pt \
  --epochs 50 --hidden-size 256 --num-layers 2

# Step 2 — fine-tune on grandmother's writings
python3 text_generator.py finetune \
  --checkpoint checkpoints/0wl_base.pt \
  --input-text grandma.txt \
  --output-checkpoint checkpoints/0wl_grandma.pt \
  --epochs 10 --lr 0.001

# Step 3 — fine-tune on letters
python3 text_generator.py finetune \
  --checkpoint checkpoints/0wl_grandma.pt \
  --input-text ../0wl-corpus.txt \
  --output-checkpoint checkpoints/0wl_letters.pt \
  --epochs 10 --lr 0.001

# Step 4 — fine-tune on Peerly poems
python3 text_generator.py finetune \
  --checkpoint checkpoints/0wl_letters.pt \
  --input-text peerly_poems.txt \
  --output-checkpoint checkpoints/0wl_final.pt \
  --epochs 15 --lr 0.001

Generating from the final model

python3 text_generator.py generate \
  --checkpoint checkpoints/0wl_final.pt \
  --prompt "Dear baka," \
  --temperature 0.8 \
  --length 800

Model architecture

  • TypeCharacter-level LSTM (2 layers, hidden size 256)
  • LibraryPyTorch ≥ 2.0
  • DeviceAuto-detects: Apple Silicon MPS → CUDA → CPU
  • Dropout0.3 (degradation trained in structurally)
  • Fine-tune LR0.001 (lower than base to avoid overwriting earlier voices)
  • CheckpointsSaved after every epoch — use any layer as a generation source
  • Bug fix appliedmodel.model_type now saved correctly in finetune (was broken in original)

Corpus balance note

Because each archive is absorbed through a separate fine-tuning pass, smaller corpora (Peerly's 40 poems, grandmother's writings) are not drowned by larger ones. Each voice leaves its trace at a different depth. Books 0+1 are the deep foundation. Grandmother is the next layer. The letters are next. Peerly arrives last and drifts closest to the surface of what the model generates.
